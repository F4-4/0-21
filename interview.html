PM Interview Notes

Personal private study notes to refresh talking points and prepare for interviews.

Scoring

Fit & Credibility

Do you understand their specific problem(s), and can you back up your background with real verifiable depth?

Execution & Proof

Can you communicate clearly, drive hard metrics, and get into the weeds of product build and analytics?

Strategy & Decision-Making

How do you evaluate trade-offs, weigh risks, and tie product choices directly to business revenue and market positioning?

Leadership & Adaptability

How do you bring people with you, resolve conflict, course correct, and learn from mistakes?

Stories

Situation: To support company goals around MAU growth, I led research combining competitive analysis, qualitative data, and user interviews. It pointed to one clear drop-off driver: high friction from traditional BIP39 seed phrases, made worse by negative sentiment around the persistent home-screen security banners. Competitors hadn't solved this either; they were still defaulting to BIP39 too.

Task: I set out to scope and launch native Passkey authentication, both to cut that top-of-funnel drop-off and free up the home-screen UI space, all without delaying our core sprint deliverables.

Action: I looked at two technical paths: smart-contract wallets, which also use passkeys but are network-dependent, versus building a custom multi-network implementation. I went with the custom path so we wouldn't be tied to the protocol team's roadmap, even though it meant giving up some of the ecosystem-developer benefits smart accounts would've brought. To keep sprint timelines intact, I negotiated to borrow an engineer from another team. From there I wrote the PRD, drove async alignment across Design, Eng, and Marketing — including getting Marketing and Design aligned on reframing the security narrative itself, so removing the banners read as "Passkeys handle this for you now" rather than us just quietly deleting a warning — and managed an external security audit with Cossack Labs.

Result: Post-launch, onboarding drop-off fell 13%, and wallet creation time dropped 92%, from over two minutes down to under ten seconds, thanks to native Apple and Google cloud backups. We also eliminated the security banners entirely. Altogether that drove a roughly 4% lift in overall MAU.

Situation: A company director approached me with an impromptu ask to build a feature aimed at growing the Tezos ecosystem and alleviating user anxiety around network payments. While total transaction volume was roughly one hundred thousand per day, user research revealed stress about sending payments and making test transactions to avoid losses due to incorrect details. I initially pushed back based on the feature's value at the time relative to our existing priorities, noting this would only work P2P and that exchanges accounted for over 90% of all transfers. The director clarified he wanted me to carry out the work regardless.

Task: As the Product Manager, I was accountable for discovery, requirements definition, and stakeholder alignment on this bet.

Action: I negotiated with an adjacent team to borrow a protocol specialist, so my own delivery timelines on existing priorities wouldn't be put at risk, and designed a time-boxed MVP, which was a Michelson contract on Tezos available as a web app to the broader network to ensure maximum access.

Result: At the time, we had direct influence and access to fifteen hundred MAU processing about one hundred transactions daily, and adoption of the escrow feature started at about five transactions per day, though, as suspected, P2P transactions were the primary use case so reach was naturally limited. The biggest takeaway from this experience was the value of time-boxing, allowing us to test a director-driven bet in a controlled way to validate demand without overwhelming our core priorities. Had this been a success I would have expanded the feature to other networks we supported, but ultimately it did not move the needle — as the crypto bear market continued, activity eventually dropped to zero, and while the feature still technically operates today, no one uses it.

Situation: Blockchain.com's Bitcoin block explorer was the world's largest, driving 300,000+ daily unique visitors. Though it was our biggest organic top-of-funnel asset, it was commercially underperforming, converting to our brokerage at just ~0.01%, since users treated it solely as a single-use transaction checker.

Task: My task was to turn the explorer from a passive utility into a functioning acquisition channel without compromising the trust that made it the most-used tool of its kind.

Action: I directed Data Science to build a tracking dashboard, which found 96% of traffic landed on just two page types, 82% Bitcoin transactions, 14% Bitcoin addresses, with shallow depth at 2.4 pages per session, and that most traffic came direct from third-party wallet apps, so I reached out to wallet providers to formalise and establish relationships. I also deployed one-question micro-surveys on transaction pages, which surfaced a clear brand perception gap users told us they were not aware Blockchain.com had a brokerage. From there I redesigned navigation on desktop and mobile to expose hidden pages, and added sticky header sign-up CTAs and native first-party ads directly on primary transaction pages, which permanently lifted baseline site conversion from 0.01% to 0.3%, a 30x increase. To capitalise on the Bitcoin Halving, I pitched an event-driven promo, a $10 bonus on a $100 trade via a live countdown hub. Finance and Marketing pushed back over bonus hunters and low-intent traffic, so I won sign-off by modeling a $7 CPA (after fees) against our $100+ paid channel benchmark, gating the bonus behind a $100 trade minimum to guarantee fee revenue and filter for intent, and leveraging our 30x baseline lift as proof of commercial viability.

Result: We doubled session depth from 2.4 to 4.7 pages per session. The Halving countdown hub gained massive viral distribution across Twitter, YouTube livestreams, and major news outlets, and the campaign page converted at ~7%, bringing in 8,000 new sign-ups at a ~$7 CPA and generating $2.2M+ in trading volume during the campaign window. More lastingly, it replaced internal cross-functional skepticism with a repeatable, event-driven growth flywheel that was adopted for future campaigns.

Themes

1. Strategy

  • How do you define product strategy for a new initiative, and how do you align it with overall business objectives?

Strategy

  • Market & Moat: Analyze market dynamics, competitor positioning, and unique advantages.
  • Define: Define explicit what to do and what NOT to do trade-offs.
  • Mapping: Translate vision into multi-horizon outcomes (Now / Next / Later).
  • Link: Connect feature direction directly to monetization, retention, or growth loops.

2. Discovery

  • How do you approach discovery? Tell me about a time you validated a user problem and how you identified it?

Discovery

  • Framing: Define the core user pain point and business impact before touching solutions.
  • Insights: Combine quant (funnels, cohort data) with qual (user interviews, support tickets).
  • De-risking: Test assumptions early via low-fi prototypes, landing page tests, or concierge MVPs.
  • Alignment: Partner with Design & Eng to evaluate value, usability, feasibility, and viability.

3. Prioritising

  • How do you arrange priorities? Tell me about a tradeoff you have made under pressure and roadmap planning.

Prioritising

  • Structure: Apply scoring frameworks to evaluate objectively (RICE, Value vs. Effort).
  • Alignment: Map every initiative back to top-level business goals and North Star metrics.
  • Tradeoffs: Balance user value, tech debt, speed-to-market and revenue impacts.
  • Pushback: Say "no" or "later" with clear rationale, alternatives and evidence.

4. Influencing

  • How do you influence stakeholders? Tell me about a time you had to convince someone and how you handled it.

Influencing

  • Empathy: What does engineering, design, legal, marketing care about? (tech debt, compliance...).
  • Data: Remove personal opinion, bring evidence (research, analytics, user feedback...).
  • Alignment: How does it map to company goals, OKRs, outcomes?
  • Trade-offs: Willing to compromise (scope / timing) to achieve results and maintain relationships.

5. Conflicts

  • How do you handle conflict? Tell me about a time you had a disagreement or received pushback, and handled conflicting priorities.

Conflicts

  • Empathy: uncover the underlying incentives and core concerns behind the pushback.
  • Objective Framing: shift debates from personal opinions to shared metrics, data, and user value.
  • Collaborative Trade-offs: use frameworks (e.g. RICE, trade-off matrix) to negotiate scope vs. speed.
  • Disagree and Commit: align on a path forward with clear ownership, even if full consensus isn't hit.

6. Metrics

  • How do you define success metrics for a feature, and what do you do when a metric moves in the wrong direction?

Metrics

  • Metric Tree: Define North Star, primary success metrics, and counter/guardrail metrics.
  • Instrumentation: Partner with data/engineering early to ensure clean telemetry and event tracking.
  • Root Cause: Use cohort segmentation and funnels to isolate drop-off drivers.
  • Thresholds: Define clear pre-launch thresholds for rolling out, iterating, or killing features.

7. Evaluating

  • How do you measure success? Tell me about a time you evaluated a feature's performance and what actions you took based on the results.

Evaluating

  • Success Metrics: Track adoption, retention, and North Star impact against baseline goals.
  • Cohort Analysis: Evaluate user segment behavior over time to verify sustained value.
  • Feedback Loops: Gather post-launch qualitative feedback to capture unexpected edge cases.
  • Iteration: Decide whether to double down, pivot, or sunset based on actual ROI.

8. Diagnosing

  • How do you diagnose what went wrong? Walk me through your step-by-step diagnostic process for a problem you faced.

Diagnosing

  • Framing: Define the core user pain point and business impact before touching solutions.
  • Insights: Combine quant (funnels, cohort data) with qual (user interviews, support tickets).
  • De-risking: Test assumptions early via low-fi prototypes, landing page tests, or concierge MVPs.
  • Alignment: Partner with Design & Eng to evaluate value, usability, feasibility, and viability.

9. Emergency

  • How do you handle emergencies? Tell me about an incident you managed under pressure.

Emergency

  • Rapid Triage & Containment stop the bleeding first (e.g., feature flag, rollback) before debugging.
  • Incident Command & Comms establish a single source of truth, run status syncs, and manage stakeholders.
  • Customer First focus on blast radius, user impact, and transparent external communication.
  • Post-Incident Prevention run a blameless retro to build systemic guardrails and prevent recurrence.

10. Reflecting

  • How do you reflect on work? Tell me about a failure or mistake you learned from, and what you'd do differently looking back.

Reflecting

  • Blameless Reviews: Focus on systemic causes and process fixes rather than pointing fingers.
  • Data vs. Hypotheses: Dissect gaps between expected outcomes and actual user behavior.
  • Feedback: Solicit structured feedback from eng, design, and users (e.g., retro cadences).
  • Awareness: Demonstrate humility, ownership, and immediate iteration.
